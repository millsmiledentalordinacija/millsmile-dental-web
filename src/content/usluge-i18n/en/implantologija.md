## What are dental implants?

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth and can serve as support for a crown, a bridge or another prosthetic structure.

Planning includes an assessment of oral health, the available bone and the options for prosthetic restoration. Not all patients are candidates for the same type of treatment.

The aim is to achieve a stable, functional and aesthetically harmonious solution according to your individual needs.

## Replacing one or more missing teeth

When a single tooth is missing, an implant can support an individual crown. When several teeth are missing, an implant-supported bridge or another prosthetic solution can be considered. The choice depends on the condition of the neighbouring teeth, the bone, the bite and your needs.

## All-on-4, All-on-6 and All-on-X – fixed teeth on implants

If you are missing most or all of the teeth in one jaw, one of the options may be a fixed restoration supported by several implants.

**All-on-4** refers to the concept of replacing the teeth of a whole jaw on four implants, while **All-on-6** uses six implants. **All-on-X** is a broader name for such solutions, in which the number and position of implants are adapted to the anatomical conditions and the treatment plan.

The number of implants is not determined by the name of the procedure alone. It is important to assess bone quality and quantity, load distribution, bite and other clinical factors.

### Fixed teeth within a shorter period after the procedure

In certain cases a temporary fixed restoration can be made within a relatively short period after implant placement. **This does not mean that fixed teeth are made on the same day.** Whether and when it can be fitted depends on implant stability, bone condition and the individual treatment plan. The final restoration is planned after an appropriate healing phase.

## What does implant treatment involve?

**1. Examination and diagnostics.** The condition of the mouth, the bite and the bone are assessed, with appropriate radiological diagnostics.

**2. Individual plan.** The treatment options, the planned stages of treatment and the prosthetic solution are agreed.

**3. Implant placement.** The procedure is usually carried out under local anaesthesia, with instructions for the period afterwards.

**4. Healing and prosthetic work.** Depending on clinical conditions, a temporary solution, check-ups and the final restoration are planned.

## Recovery and maintenance

Swelling, sensitivity and discomfort are possible after the procedure, usually most noticeable in the first days. Bone healing takes longer than the initial recovery. Daily oral hygiene, regular check-ups and following individual instructions are important for the long-term stability of implants.
